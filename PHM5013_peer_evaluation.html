Peer Allocation of Marks
Required

Rate each team member's contribution to the project on a scale of 0 to 100 (including yourself), where 100 represents full contribution. Each member's scores will be averaged across all evaluators to produce a scaling factor (out of 100) applied directly to the group mark.

Worked Example: How Your Allocation Affects Marks
Suppose your group scores 85 / 100 on the project. Each evaluator (including self) rates every member's contribution from 0 to 100. The scaling factor for each member is their average PAM score divided by 100.
Member Avg Contribution Score Scaling Factor Calculation Final Mark
Alice 93 93 / 100 = 0.93 85 × 0.93 79
Bob 70 70 / 100 = 0.70 85 × 0.70 60
Carol 40 40 / 100 = 0.40 85 × 0.40 34
A score of 100 means the member contributed fully and receives the full group mark. Lower scores reduce the final mark proportionally. For example, an average contribution score of 70 means the member receives 70% of the group mark.
Are you sure all contributions were equal?
Equal point allocation is uncommon. In most teams, members contribute differently across research, writing, analysis, and coordination. Please reconsider whether your allocation truly reflects each person's contribution.
