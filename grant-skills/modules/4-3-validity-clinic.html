The validity clinic

A paper has landed in your inbox for review. You have four questions you can put to the authors. There are more than four things wrong with it.

About 15 minutes. One case, no steps. Everything you write stays on this device.

This module is not built like the others. There is no pre-check, no eight steps and no step list down the side, because the skill here is not one you can look up and then recite. It is a judgement made under a budget, so the module is one continuous case and the budget is real.

You are Reviewer 2

An education journal has sent you the abstract below. In a real review you cannot interrogate everything: you read once, you notice what you notice, and the questions you choose to raise are the review. So you get four probes. Spend them.

Each probe is a question you put to the authors. Some will turn up something. Some will turn up nothing, and will still have cost you a probe. That is the exercise.

The two questions that keep paying

Everything you just spent probes on comes down to two habits, and they are worth more than a memorised list of threat names.

Ask what the comparison is. Then ask what the measure would record if your favourite explanation were false.

The first question catches the historical control, the changed examination paper and the students who left. All three are the same mistake wearing different clothes: something other than the intervention differed between the two numbers being compared.

The second catches the quiet one. An assignment mark taken with the assistant switched on would look exactly the same whether students had learned more or learned nothing and delegated better. A measure that cannot tell those two apart cannot support a claim about either.

Watch out

The threats with names, history, maturation, instrumentation, selection, attrition, are useful for writing the limitations paragraph. They are not much use for finding the problem in the first place. The two questions above find it faster, and you can ask them of your own design before you collect anything.

Now your own study

Turn the same two questions on your own project, while it is still cheap to change. This is saved with the rest of your draft.
