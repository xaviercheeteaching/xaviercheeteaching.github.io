Module 5.1 of Part 5

Ethics review for education studies at NUS

It is not on the grant form. It is the thing that stops the money, and it has to happen before you teach a single class of the study.

About 25 minutes. Eight steps. Everything you write stays on this device.

Step 1

About 3 minutes

Why this matters

Read the TEG application form from top to bottom and you will not find an ethics section. There is no box, no attachment, no question about participants. Ethics is entirely post-award. So the natural thing to think is that it comes later, when there is time.

Here is what happens if you do. The approval documents have to reach CTLT before your funds are released, so nothing is spent until they arrive. If approval has not been submitted by 30 September, the grant lapses. And if you have already started collecting data, the committee will not review the study at all, because it does not conduct retrospective reviews. At that point the study is not delayed. It is unpublishable.

So the form that has no ethics section is the form whose money does not arrive without one. That is the whole reason this module sits where it does.

What you will be able to do

Say which route your study takes, name the exemption category it falls in, and defend that choice against the one question that decides it.

Before the NUS specifics

Three minutes on why anyone reviews education research at all. This one is from an American university, so the committee names, the forms and the procedure it describes are not ours. Take the reasoning and leave the process; everything after this step is the NUS version.

Step 2

About 3 minutes

Where you stand

Six questions. This is not a grade. It is here so that you and we can see what the next twenty minutes actually add. You will meet the same six questions again at step 7, and you will see the answers then.

For each one, say how sure you are. This is a module where people are confident and wrong more often than they are unsure and wrong, so the confidence reading is worth more here than the score.

Step 3

About 5 minutes

The idea

It goes to LACE, not to NUS-IRB

Education research at NUS does not normally go to NUS-IRB. It goes to LACE, the Learning and Analytics Committee on Ethics, which is a Departmental Ethics Review Committee, or DERC, set up by CTLT and ALSET. You submit through the DREAM app. You do not use iRIMS-IRB; that system is for studies that need full NUS-IRB review. The committee's address is lace-derc@nus.edu.sg.

Memory hook: DREAM for a departmental study. iRIMS-IRB only when it has to go all the way to NUS-IRB.

Six conditions decide whether you can use that route

Faculty research qualifies for departmental review when all six hold. It counts as research. It is excluded from the Human Biomedical Research Act. It is minimal risk and qualifies for an Exemption. It does not involve vulnerable populations, deception or sensitive topics. There is no medical device. And it is unfunded, self-funded, or funded by an internal NUS grant.

That last one is the one people worry about, and it is the one that is fine. A TEG award is an internal NUS grant, so it qualifies. External or industry funding does not.

Fail any of the other five and the study leaves this route and goes to NUS-IRB. Interviews about failure and mental health, a study that hides its purpose from the students, work with a population that cannot freely refuse: any one of those, on its own, is enough.

Then one of three exemption categories

Once you are inside departmental review, you name a category. There are three, and picking the right one is most of the application.

The three exemption categories, and what each one costs you
Category What it covers What it demands
1A Normal educational practices in established educational settings: instructional strategies, comparing instructional techniques, curricula, classroom management. Pre- and post-tests or surveys around an intervention are included. That no analysis of academic grades will be involved. This is stated in the category itself, and it is the line that decides everything.
1B Educational research that does analyse grades. Five criteria, all of which must hold. Two of the five bite. The aim must be to assess class or programme effectiveness or to help students learn better. And prospective consent must be obtained from all students for the use of their grades and data.
1C Anonymous or de-identified student feedback, or evaluation of instruction. If consent is not being obtained, a waiver of informed consent has to be applied for. It is not automatic because the data is anonymous.

The grades question is the fork

Everything above narrows to one question. Will you analyse academic grades?

Say no and you are in 1A, and the application is light. Say yes and you are in 1B, and you must get prospective consent from every student, before the fact, for the use of their grades. Not consent at the end of term when the marks are safely released. Before.

This is not a paperwork detail. It changes what you can promise the TEG panel, because the outcome a panel most wants to see is usually an examination result, and the outcome that keeps you in 1A usually is not. Module 5.2 is about how that consent is actually taken when you are the person marking the work.

Memory hook: touch grades, and every student has to say yes first.

Check the current version

The three exemption categories on this page are taken from a copy of the LACE document dated 25 August 2020. A newer version, dated 2 March 2026, sits on NUS SharePoint and could not be reached when this page was written. The 1A, 1B and 1C structure is very likely unchanged, but the exact wording may have moved. Read the current PDF before you name your category, and write to lace-derc@nus.edu.sg if you cannot find it.

There is no retrospective review

The committee's own words: it "does not conduct retrospective reviews on research/studies that have commenced or have already been completed." And the consequence it names: "papers arising from the study/research may not be accepted for publication in a journal or presentation at an international conference."

Read that twice. This is not a rule about being told off. It is a rule about whether the work exists. A semester of teaching, a dataset you are pleased with, and no route to a journal.

The sheet you hand the students

You need a Participant Information Sheet and Consent Form, the PIS and CF. One trap here catches almost everyone: for a study approved by a departmental committee, the sheet must carry the DERC's contact details, not NUS-IRB's. Templates float around with the NUS-IRB address already in them. A student who has a complaint should be able to reach the committee that actually saw the study.

Watch out

Two ideas get confused here and they are separate. Whether your study needs review is one question. Which category it falls in is another, and you only reach it once the first is settled. A study can be perfectly ordinary teaching and still need an application, because what needs approval is the research use of the data, not the teaching.

Worked example

One case, worked in full. Write your own answer first, then reveal. The writing is what makes this work, so do not skip it.

Step 5

About 6 minutes

Practice

First: the order of the questions

These are the seven questions that get you from "I have an idea" to a named category. They are shuffled. Put them in the order you actually have to ask them, using the Up and Down buttons, then check.

Then: seven real studies

Seven cards, four bins. The fourth bin is not a way out: putting a study there is a claim that it cannot use departmental review at all and has to go to NUS-IRB, and you should only make that claim on purpose.

Step 6

About 4 minutes

Your study

Now your own project. What you write here is saved on this device and nowhere else, and it carries forward to the other modules. The first box goes into your ethics application, so write it as you would write it there.

If you cannot decide between 1A and 1B, that usually means the design is not settled yet, not that the rules are unclear. Answer the grades question first and the category follows.

Step 7

About 2 minutes

Check again

The same six questions, in the same order. Answer them again, and say how sure you are again. This time you get the answers, your score before and after, and whether your confidence matched your accuracy.
